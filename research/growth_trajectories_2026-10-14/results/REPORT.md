## Methods and limits

Baseline: ≥21 non-null download observations, span ≥28 days, date coverage ≥0.8, maximum date spacing ≤3 days. Strict: span ≥42, date and download coverage ≥0.95, spacing ≤1 day. Direction: ≥4 weekly blocks with ≥4 measured values each, ≥75% successive block median changes in the stated direction, endpoint absolute change ≥10,000 and relative change ≥25%. Stronger shape: ≥80%, ≥100,000 and ≥50%. Interior peak: measured maximum strictly inside metric endpoints, both rise and fall ≥10,000 and ≥25% of peak (stronger: 100,000 and 50%). Flat weekly comparisons remain in the denominator. Thresholds are pragmatic and ambiguous near boundaries.
Weekly blocks are anchored at each repository’s first observed date; sparse blocks are omitted, so block comparisons need not be consecutive weeks. Total variation uses only adjacent valid endpoints; nonzero sign reversal counts can span flat or missing intervals and are screening evidence, not proof of a sustained reversal. Interior peak rise/fall is endpoint evidence, not proof of a unique spike or persistent reversal. No interpolation or extrapolation is performed.
47 days of overlapping rolling counts support descriptive short-window patterns, not established lifecycle classes or predictive reliability. Rolling 30-day counts overlap; rates are changes in that rolling metric, never actual daily downloads. No cumulative downloads can be derived. Counts reflect format-dependent file requests, not unique users. Exact repository IDs remain separate. Creation dates do not recover unobserved early histories. Endpoint changes may use shorter metric-specific periods when endpoints are missing.
Next research: prospectively defined early prediction with time-separated validation; persistence/survival with censoring and longer follow-up; category comparisons with explicit sampling and confounding controls. None is implemented here.
